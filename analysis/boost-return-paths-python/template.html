Where the boost converter's currents actually flow

The switching loop is routed through the bottom layer because the output capacitor and the MT3608 ground pin sit on two separate top-layer copper islands. The feedback divider's ground sits in the middle of that detour. DC drops are negligible; the problems are loop area and ground noise at the 1.2 MHz switching edges.

Findings, most severe first

Each item links to the board view where it shows up. Percentages for the switching and input loops are shares of a 1 A loop current; the DC view uses a 1 A load.

    If you respin it

    1. Close the switching loop on the top layer. Place C3 so its ground pad lands beside IC1 pin 2 and D1's cathode, pour solid copper between them and stitch C3− to the bottom with 2–4 vias.
    2. Clear the bottom layer under the converter. Move the BAT+/BAT− pair to the top or around the edge, or put the battery pads next to L1 and Q1, so the bottom is one unbroken ground under L1, D1, IC1 and C3.
    3. Kelvin the feedback. R2/R3 next to IC1 pin 3, R3's ground straight to IC1 pin 2 on its own trace, 5 V sense taken from C3+ or J2.
    4. Bypass IN. 1–4.7 µF X7R from pin 5 to pin 2 at the package.
    5. Shorten the SW node. D1's anode at IC1 pin 1, SW connection as wide as the pad, C1 against L1 pin 1 with its ground on IC1's copper.

    What already works

    • The battery pair is tight. BAT+ and BAT− run side by side 2.5 mm apart, so the DC battery loop encloses little area.
    • The output path is short and wide. D1 → C3 → J2 is 1.27 mm copper over about 8 mm.
    • SW and FB leave the IC on opposite sides. The SW copper stays above and left of IC1; the FB trace leaves downwards.
    • IC1 pin 2 has three vias. Without them island A would hang on C4's single via.
    • DC ground offsets are negligible. At 1 A out, R3's ground sits 0.44 mV above IC1's, which moves the output by −3.3 mV.

    How the currents were computed

    • Copper as fabricated. Both copper layers come from the 2019-01-18 production Gerbers (EAGLE 9.2.2), rasterised at 0.05 mm. Net names, pads and traces come from the KiCad-2019 project, whose tracks match the Gerbers.
    • Resistive current spreading. The ground copper of both layers is solved as a sheet network: 35 µm copper (0.49 mΩ/□), vias with 0.35 mm drill and 20 µm plating (1.25 mΩ each), plated pins as larger barrels. Currents are injected and removed at the pad areas.
    • Operating point. Ideal converter, 3.7 V cell, 5.1 V out, 1 A load: 1.5 A from the cell and 0.5 A average switch current out of IC1 pin 2.
    • Limits. At switching-edge frequencies the return current crowds closer to the forward path than a DC solution shows, so the field overstates spreading. It still has to use the same islands and vias, which is why loop area is given as a range: 93 mm² for the tightest possible route through the J3 vias, 180 mm² for the DC spread (magnetic moment of the solved current).
    • KiCad check. Re-running the solve on KiCad's own zone fill gives 3.8 mΩ instead of 4.4 mΩ and R3 at 73 % instead of 68 %. The conclusions are the same for both.
